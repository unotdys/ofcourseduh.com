Episode 1 · Welcome to Gigs and Wigs: Swim Week, UFOs and the Art of Overpacking

23:38 · Open on YouTube

It's our inaugural episode! Two celebrity hairstylists, one podcast, and every story we usually only tell behind the chair.

Meet your hosts. Brandon Liberati does hair and wigs for Bravo, red carpets and rock stars. Vicki Casciola is "the Transformer" and an extension queen with 25‑plus years behind the chair. In their very first episode, Brandon and his "hair wife" get into why being your authentic self is polarizing (and why that's the point), how they met at a hair show in Orlando, and why the hair world keeps everyone in separate lanes.

Then it's the month in gigs. Vicki: Miami Swim Week, four shows in 48 hours, and the year of the booty. Brandon: Netflix Is A Joke's Pee‑wee's Playhouse 40th (hello, Danny Elfman), a desert night of UFO‑spotting with the Ancient Aliens crowd, and the European summer tour with Devo and The B‑52's he leaves for next week. They also cover how you pack for that many cities (the Sudoku method, tested), every Zara in London, ABBA Voyage, and what's next for their education platform Glam Mob, Starship Salon and their agency Cactus Artists. 💇✨

Episode 2 · The Transformer & the Bravo-verse: How Brandon and Vicki Got Into Hair

8:09 · Open on YouTube

Extensions at age ten in Las Vegas, an African American hair school in the Tenderloin, and a first week in LA that changed everything. These are the stories behind the stylists.

Before the red carpets and the reality TV, there were two kids who just had to do hair. In this episode of Gigs and Wigs, Brandon Liberati turns the mic on his "hair wife" Vicki Casciola. She was braiding hair at five and installing extensions at ten in Vegas (glue choice: questionable). By 15 she was "the Transformer", working out of the back room with the house mom. Then Vicki asks Brandon how he ended up next to her: doing his sisters' hair in Utah, learning wigs and weaves in San Francisco, doing hair for Silicon Valley clients headed to the Oscars, and meeting his husband in his first week in LA. That's how he landed in the Bravo‑verse, from RuPaul's Drag U to the Food Network to Shahs of Sunset.

It's a love letter to saying yes to everything, finding your tribe, and the music that gets you through. Hair took them on one beautiful journey. Thelma and Louise, minus the cliff. 💇✨

Episode 3 · TMI, Protest Songs and Punk-Rock Bangs with Jane Wiedlin of The Go-Go's

24:53 · Open on YouTube

A Go-Go, a memoir called TMI, and an album of protest songs. Jane Wiedlin pulls up a chair at the salon.

Brandon has been "a Jane" since forever, and today she's in the chair. Jane Wiedlin of The Go‑Go's joins Brandon Liberati and Vicki Casciola to talk about her memoir TMI. It doesn't hold back: a dad in a prisoner‑of‑war camp, living in shacks and foreign countries, a stint in a BDSM dungeon, and the Rock Hall bookends. She also talks about her new record I Protest, an album of contemporary protest‑song covers she produced herself with a tight crew of friends, and "Yer Dihh", her song with Margaret Cho (you'll know what it's about by the first two words).

Plus: a book tour planned as "an evening in the salon of Jane Wiedlin", the 4 a.m. party photo that became her cover, gigs with Gina Schock, singing "Rush Hour" live, being shadow‑banned at 103K followers, and why she does not want a round haircut. Brandon tells the story of the day a Betty White prank show caught him on Runyon Canyon. Then everyone heads off to get Jane's hair did. 💇✨

Episode 4 · Just Run: Entrepreneurs, Pivots and a Surprise Visit from Steve Young

15:40 · Open on YouTube

Two hairstylists, one very loaded word (entrepreneur), and a Hall of Fame quarterback who walks in for a haircut.

Brandon and Vicki are back from Vegas (Benson Boone, a stadium of screaming fans, and a Morrissey run that was cancelled in true Morrissey fashion) and get into the least glamorous part of the job: being an entrepreneur. Starship, Cactus Artists, Glam Mob: they opened them all without a single duck in a row, and learned that it isn't about being the smartest person in the room, it's about problem‑solving. Plus why you never put all your eggs in one basket, and why so many great hairstylists started out in healthcare.

Then Brandon's client Steve Young walks in for his haircut, right on cue. The football great turned investor (he's with Beauty Industry Group, the company behind some of the biggest hair-extension brands) talks about pivoting: bring everything you've got, have the humility to admit you're starting over, and the one piece of advice his childhood hero Roger Staubach gave him. "Run." After the haircut, Brandon and Vicki wrap up on why your reputation is your currency. 💇✨

Episode 5 · Influencers Out, Experts In: Social Media, with a Call from Naomi Grossman

27:55 · Open on YouTube

Social media: the thing that keeps us all up at night. Plus a FaceTime call to American Horror Story's Naomi Grossman, live from set.

Vicki takes forever to post (it has to be perfect). Brandon posts the way he plays Vegas: max bet, hit, walk away. Together they get into the curse (comparing yourself, being your own PR, marketing and sales team) and the blessing (no more carrying a portfolio book), why brands are moving from influencers to experts, why raw behind‑the‑scenes beats a perfectly curated doll shoot, and why the people you follow should leave you inspired, not alienated.

Then they FaceTime their friend and longtime client Naomi Grossman, Pepper from American Horror Story, on the set of her new indie film. She talks about being "the boss" this time (number two on the call sheet), her four seasons as Pepper, filming her live show American Whore Story on spec, whether casting directors really check your follower count, and yoga hair. Brandon and Vicki's verdict: scroll past people who suck. 📱✨

Episode 6 · Everybody Needs a Fred in Their Life: Fred Schneider of The B-52's

16:34 · Open on YouTube

Love Shack plays at every wedding on earth. Today, the voice behind it calls in.

Brandon is just back from The B‑52's European summer tour (Berlin was his favorite), and Vicki has done hair couture pieces for the band too. They cover why Love Shack is a wedding staple, where the band's name really comes from (it's a beehive, not a bomber), the acting‑class improv game named after Fred, and one piece of advice: never run up to Fred for a photo.

Then they call Fred Schneider himself. He talks about Fred Says and the dance he invented, Wig on a Stick, the Mexico City show with metal dressing rooms (and Hanson next door), life on tour, the memes he sends Brandon every morning, the Christmas album that's being re‑released, and why small personal projects keep artists going. Vicki sums it up: everybody needs a Fred in their life. 🎶✨
